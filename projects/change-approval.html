Change-Approval Lead-Time Reduction

Re-engineered the change-request lifecycle for a business-critical clinical application — clearer impact analysis and traceability cut CR lead time from 3–4 days to under 24 hours and supported on-time global releases across multiple markets.

RoleProduct Technical Advisor
OrganizationIQVIA R&DS
TypeProcess & workflow redesign

The problem

Change requests for a business-critical clinical application were spending three to four days in approval — long enough to jeopardise on-time global releases and delay resolution of the data issues that depended on them. In a regulated GxP context, the delay wasn't just inconvenient; it created real risk to release commitments across multiple markets.

The approach

  • Mapped the existing CR flow end-to-end and pinpointed where impact analysis and traceability gaps forced re-work.
  • Restructured the lifecycle around clear intake, structured impact analysis, and explicit timeline estimation — so reviewers had what they needed on the first pass.
  • Designed an approval workflow that preserved GxP compliance while removing avoidable hops between reviewers.
  • Introduced traceability at each stage so audit and rollback paths remained intact.
  • Aligned stakeholders across product, engineering, QA, and customer teams on the new criteria and delivery negotiation.

Impact

  • Change-request lead time reduced from 3–4 days to under 24 hours.
  • Supported on-time global releases across multiple markets.
  • Accelerated resolution of data issues that were previously blocked in the queue.

What I learned

  • In regulated environments, faster and safer aren't opposites — clarity of criteria is what removes the delay.
  • The most valuable part of a lifecycle redesign is the shared language it creates across product, engineering, and QA.
  • Traceability is a feature: pair every workflow change with the artefacts audit will actually ask for.
